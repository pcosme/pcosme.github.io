Research projects

Surface dynamics in odd fluids

In fluids with broken parity/time-reversal-simmetry one can have responses transverse to the currents. In particular, odd viscosity unlocks stress perpendicular to the strain rate without dissipation. This gives free surfaces a chiral response. we study the interplay of such viscous behaviour with surface tension in different setting such capillary waves and droplet normal modes. Our results reveal how chirality separates counter-propagating modes, redistributes angular momentum, and alters nonlinear wave interactions.

Synchronisation and phase transitions in rotary microswimmers

Chiral spinning microswimmers interact with their neighbours via their rotation; not only advecting them but also favouring some alignment. by the flagellar dynamics of uniflagellate Chlamydomonas reinhardtii, this project explores when many such oscillators synchronise and how collective polar, nematic, or chiral phases emerge. Generalised XY and high harmonic Kuramoto models connect microscopic couplings to phase transitions, defects, and large-scale order.

Angular-momentum transport in chiral fluids

Chiral fluids possess internal rotational degrees of freedom, so angular momentum and directional order parameter can be transported and interact wit the remaining (quasi) conserved quantities. We develop continuum and kinetic descriptions of this exchange, including micropolar stresses, couple stresses, and physically consistent boundary conditions. The aim is to identify observable signatures in confined flows, interfaces, and waves.

Chemo-rheology of active droplets

Active droplets combine internally generated stresses with chemical reactions that may themselves oscillate. This project studies the feedback loop between chemical state, active stress, flow, and droplet shape: how chemical rhythms reshape the effective rheology and how mechanics, in turn, reorganises those rhythms. Reduced models and simulations expose the onset of oscillatory, symmetry-breaking, and self-organised states.

Topological interfacial modes in electron hydrodynamics

Interfaces between electron fluids can guide collective modes whose propagation depends on the topology of the Hamiltonian and the properties of the interfacial transition. This project asks when those modes become localised and directional, and whichever are topological protected. Efectively bridgind electronic hydrodynamics, plasmonics, and topological modes physics. We were able to obtain the spectrum of bound states and observe that besides compactification odd viscosity introduces significant changes on the allowed modes
